# Personal Productivity Framework

This framework is designed for users who thrive on high-level conceptual understanding but struggle with task execution due to polarized procrastination triggers: tasks that are too easy (boredom) or too complex/ambiguous (friction).

## Core Principles
1. **Academic Tax Minimization**: Treat university/standard assessments as "taxes" to be paid with minimum effort (80/20 mastery) to preserve energy for high-value projects.
2. **Procrastination Analysis**: Identify the trigger before applying the fix.
   - **Boredom (Easy Tasks)**: Requires engagement, complexity scaling, or automated/streamlined execution.
   - **Ambiguity (Hard Tasks)**: Requires micro-stepping, scaffolding, and time-boxed struggle.

## Procedures

### 1. Hard-Task Scaffolding & The "Meta-Tool" Avoidance Trap
When tackling genuinely difficult engineering or learning topics:
- **Beware the Meta-Tool Trap**: Building tools *about* learning or productivity (e.g. custom prompt harnesses, complex subagent extensions, or framework wrappers) is often a sneaky form of high-friction avoidance. It provides an illusion of progress while delaying actual fundamental practice. Always prioritize direct, low-friction practice on the core skill (e.g. writing raw Java DSA code) over building tooling around it.
- **Vibe-Coding to First-Principles Transition**: When moving away from AI-generated code to authentic deep competence, do not attempt to rebuild large complex systems from scratch manually right away (which creates massive ambiguity friction). Break fundamental concepts into plain-English, bite-sized micro-steps and small reproducible exercises.
- **Micro-stepping**: Break the task until the first step requires almost zero effort (e.g., "write the first sentence of a summary" vs "learn transformers").
- **Time-Boxed Struggle (25-30 mins)**: Commit only to struggling with the problem until the timer rings. Do not aim for completion, only for active engagement.
- **AI Rubber-ducking**: Use interaction as a primary scaffolding tool to break down barriers when comprehension hits a wall.
- **Reframe Confusion**: Treat confusion not as a failure, but as the primary indicator of active learning.

### 2. Household Environment Adaptation & "Stealth Scheduling"
When designing routines for users living in high-friction, intrusive, or non-supportive home environments:
- **The "Coding Shield" (Social Invisibility)**: In households unsupportive of creative hobbies (art, drawing, writing, creative coding), laptop coding and technical problem solving appear externally as "academic study" and are left alone. Schedule technical/coding tasks during monitored or semi-public family windows (e.g. evening living room or shared spaces).
- **Physical vs. Screen Artifact Asymmetry**: Physical tools (sketchbooks, drawing tablets, physical pen-and-paper journals) draw immediate parental suspicion and questioning ("Why aren't you studying?"). Keep physical pen-and-paper journaling and drawing strictly isolated to guaranteed private windows (e.g. midnight door-closed sanctuary).
- **Noise Storm Management & Spatial Relocation**: Identify hours where common areas or bedrooms are hijacked by loud TV/phone noise (e.g. 7:00 PM family arrival). Never schedule high-cognitive-load problem solving during noise storms; designate these windows for relocation, dinner, passive learning (reading docs, listening to podcasts), or low-stakes leisure.
- **Sleep Consolidation vs. Late-Night Escapism**: Late-night sessions (1:00 AM – 4:00 AM) frequently develop as an escape mechanism to obtain autonomy and quiet. Fix daytime fatigue by capping afternoon naps (30–90 min max), moving deep-work blocks to late evening (8:30 PM – 11:30 PM), and targeting nighttime sleep (~12:00 AM – 1:00 AM).

### 3. Schedule Formatting & Dynamic Container Architecture
- **Time Format**: Always present daily schedules, matrices, and time blocks using a **12-hour clock (AM/PM)**.
- **Modular 3-Container Model vs. Rigid Static Days**: Avoid static identical schedules across weekdays when dismissal times vary (e.g. 1:00 PM vs. 4:00 PM). Structure available time into three swappable containers:
  1. *Cash & Hackathon Engine*: Rapid sprints for bounties, online hackathons, and paid builds.
  2. *Core CS & DSA Mastery*: First-principles problem solving (Solution-First method, NeetCode roadmap).
  3. *Academic Tax & Assignment Buffer*: High-speed 80/20 clearance of unexpected university assignments and lab submissions.
- **Student Financial Incentive Recognition**: Do not dismiss fast-turnaround hackathons or bounties (e.g. Rise In / Web3 cash prizes) as distractions from "pure" learning. For students where small bounties ($50 / 5k INR) represent significant cash flow, explicitly allocate sprint containers for them so learning and earning do not compete destructively.
- **Respect Habit/Structure Scoping**: When a session is designated for habit architecture, planning, or routine design, do NOT prematurely push into problem solving or technical instruction. Lock the structure first.

### 4. Academic Tax Minimization
- **High-Efficiency Capping**: Identify core concepts (20%) that drive exam results (80%).
- **Just-in-Time Study**: Avoid premature/extensive study. Focus on targeted prep sprints just before assessments.

### 5. Life Organization & Context Layer Integration
- **Memory/Context Engine (Supermemory)**: Maintain an external persistent knowledge graph for projects, learning queues, and daily schedules to eliminate state re-explanation and reduce planning friction.
- **Dopamine & Coping Substitution**: Directly replace passive coping loops (doomscrolling, isolated chatbot roleplay) with structured physical/social anchors (in-person study sessions, tangible creative work like art/journaling, micro-sprinted coding sessions).
